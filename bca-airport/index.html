Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, BCA means Gustavo Rizo Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: MUBA. ICAO codes that begin with M belong to Mexico and Central America. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 20.3653° N, 74.5062° W. It stands 8 metres (26 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Guantánamo, about 77 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 16/34, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 16 is heading about 160° on the compass, and one using the other end about 340°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 8 metres above sea level, Gustavo Rizo Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 15 airports of Cuba with a height in the data, it is number 13 from the top. It is about 2,260 km north of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Within 300 km of Gustavo Rizo Airport there are 13 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. The nearest large airport is Antonio Maceo International Airport (SCU), about 145 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
