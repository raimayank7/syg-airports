Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, BHX means Birmingham Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: EGBB. ICAO codes that begin with E belong to northern Europe. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 52.4539° N, 1.7480° W. It stands 100 metres (327 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 15/33, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 15 is heading about 150° on the compass, and one using the other end about 330°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 100 metres, Birmingham Airport stands higher than about 53% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 53 airports of United Kingdom with a height in the data, it is number 7 from the top. It is about 5,830 km north of the equator.
