Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, MXH means Moro Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: AYMR. ICAO codes that begin with A belong to the western South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 6.3633° S, 143.2380° E. It stands 835 metres (2,740 feet) above sea level. The nearest town in our data is Mendi, about 52 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 09/27, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 09 is heading about 90° on the compass, and one using the other end about 270°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 835 metres, Moro Airport stands higher than about 88% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 37 airports of Papua New Guinea with a height in the data, it is number 8 from the top. It is about 710 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
