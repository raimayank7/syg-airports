Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, MRU means Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: FIMP. ICAO codes that begin with F belong to central and southern Africa. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 20.4302° S, 57.6836° E. It stands 57 metres (186 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 14/32, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 14 is heading about 140° on the compass, and one using the other end about 320°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 57 metres, Sir Seewoosagur Ramgoolam International Airport stands higher than about 45% of the 4,004 airports on this site. It is about 2,270 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
