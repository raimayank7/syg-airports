Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LSA means Losuia Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: AYKA. ICAO codes that begin with A belong to the western South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 8.5058° S, 151.0810° E. It stands 8 metres (27 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Losuia Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,630 metres (5,348 feet). That suits narrow-body jets and regional aircraft. Among the 32 airports of Papua New Guinea on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 14.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 14/32, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 14 is heading about 140° on the compass, and one using the other end about 320°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 8 metres above sea level, Losuia Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 37 airports of Papua New Guinea with a height in the data, it is number 25 from the top. It is about 950 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Losuia Airport there are 3 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest large airport is Port Moresby Jacksons International Airport (POM), about 437 km away, roughly 1 h 00 min in the air.
