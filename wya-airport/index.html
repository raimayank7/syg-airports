Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, WYA means Whyalla Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: YWHA. ICAO codes that begin with Y belong to Australia. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 33.0589° S, 137.5140° E. It stands 12 metres (41 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 17/35, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 17 is heading about 170° on the compass, and one using the other end about 350°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 12 metres above sea level, Whyalla Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 152 airports of Australia with a height in the data, it is number 108 from the top. It is about 3,680 km south of the equator.
