Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LBS means Labasa Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: NFNL. ICAO codes that begin with N belong to the South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 16.4667° S, 179.3400° E. It stands 13 metres (44 feet) above sea level.

Labasa Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,069 metres (3,507 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 7 airports of Fiji on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 4.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 13/31, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 13 is heading about 130° on the compass, and one using the other end about 310°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 13 metres above sea level, Labasa Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 16 airports of Fiji with a height in the data, it is number 7 from the top. It is about 1,830 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it
