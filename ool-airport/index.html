Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, OOL means Gold Coast Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: YBCG. ICAO codes that begin with Y belong to Australia. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 28.1660° S, 153.5066° E. It stands 6 metres (21 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 14/32, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 14 is heading about 140° on the compass, and one using the other end about 320°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 6 metres above sea level, Gold Coast Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 152 airports of Australia with a height in the data, it is number 126 from the top. It is about 3,130 km south of the equator.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Gold Coast Airport there are 7 other airports with scheduled flights, and 3 of them are within 100 km. The nearest other large airport is Brisbane International Airport (BNE), about 95 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
