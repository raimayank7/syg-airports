Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, ASD means Andros Town Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: MYAF. ICAO codes that begin with M belong to Mexico and Central America. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 24.6979° N, 77.7956° W. It stands 2 metres (5 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Nassau, about 61 km away in a straight line.

Runways

Andros Town Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,237 metres (4,058 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 22 airports of Bahamas on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 20.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 09/27, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 09 is heading about 90° on the compass, and one using the other end about 270°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 2 metres above sea level, Andros Town Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 22 airports of Bahamas with a height in the data, it is number 18 from the top. It is about 2,750 km north of the equator.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Andros Town Airport there are 20 other airports with scheduled flights, and 4 of them are within 100 km. The nearest large airport is Lynden Pindling International Airport (NAS), about 50 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
