Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LMN means Limbang Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: WBGJ. ICAO codes that begin with W belong to Indonesia, Malaysia and nearby island countries. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 4.8083° N, 115.0100° E. It stands 4 metres (14 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Miri, about 122 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 04/22, lies roughly south-west to north-east. An aircraft using the end marked 04 is heading about 40° on the compass, and one using the other end about 220°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 4 metres above sea level, Limbang Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 34 airports of Malaysia with a height in the data, it is number 30 from the top. It is about 530 km north of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
