Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, FKQ means Fakfak Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: WASF. ICAO codes that begin with W belong to Indonesia, Malaysia and nearby island countries. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 2.9205° S, 132.2670° E. It stands 141 metres (462 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 10/28, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 10 is heading about 100° on the compass, and one using the other end about 280°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 141 metres, Fakfak Airport stands higher than about 59% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 121 airports of Indonesia with a height in the data, it is number 18 from the top. It is about 320 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Fakfak Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest large airport is Pattimura International Airport (AMQ), about 472 km away, roughly 1 h 05 min in the air.
