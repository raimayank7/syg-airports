Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, MIA means Miami International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: KMIA. ICAO codes that begin with K belong to the mainland United States. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 25.7960° N, 80.2898° W. It stands 2 metres (8 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 09/27, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 09 is heading about 90° on the compass, and one using the other end about 270°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 4 runways point in 3 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 2 metres above sea level, Miami International Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 645 airports of United States with a height in the data, it is number 631 from the top. It is about 2,870 km north of the equator.
