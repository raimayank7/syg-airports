Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, THQ means Tianshui Maijishan Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: ZLTS. ICAO codes that begin with Z belong to China, Mongolia and North Korea. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 34.5601° N, 105.8603° E. It stands 1,094 metres (3,590 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 10/28, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 10 is heading about 100° on the compass, and one using the other end about 280°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 1,094 metres, Tianshui Maijishan Airport stands higher than about 91% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 212 airports of China with a height in the data, it is number 58 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 88% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 3,840 km north of the equator.
