Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, VLI means Bauerfield International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: NVVV. ICAO codes that begin with N belong to the South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 17.6993° S, 168.3200° E. It stands 21 metres (70 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 11/29, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 11 is heading about 110° on the compass, and one using the other end about 290°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. All 2 runways here point the same way, so they can be used side by side.

It is about 1,970 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
