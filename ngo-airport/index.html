Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, NGO means Chubu Centrair International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: RJGG. ICAO codes that begin with R belong to East Asia, including Japan, Korea and the Philippines. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 34.8584° N, 136.8050° E. It stands 5 metres (15 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 18/36, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 18 is heading about 180° on the compass, and one using the other end about 360°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 5 metres above sea level, Chubu Centrair International Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 83 airports of Japan with a height in the data, it is number 78 from the top. It is about 3,880 km north of the equator.

Within 300 km of Chubu Centrair International Airport there are 18 other airports with scheduled flights, and 1 of them is within 100 km. The nearest other large airport is Osaka Itami International Airport (ITM), about 125 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
