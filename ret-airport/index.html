Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, RET means Røst Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: ENRS. ICAO codes that begin with E belong to northern Europe. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 67.5265° N, 12.1016° E. It stands 2 metres (7 feet) above sea level. The nearest town in our data is Bodø, about 101 km away in a straight line.

Runways

Røst Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,032 metres (3,386 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 47 airports of Norway on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 28.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 02/20, lies roughly south to north. An aircraft using the end marked 02 is heading about 20° on the compass, and one using the other end about 200°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 2 metres above sea level, Røst Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 47 airports of Norway with a height in the data, it is the lowest. It is about 7,510 km north of the equator, inside the Arctic Circle, where the sun does not set for part of the summer and does not rise for part of the winter.
