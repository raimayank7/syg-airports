Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, ADE means Aden International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: OYAA. ICAO codes that begin with O belong to the Middle East, Pakistan and Afghanistan. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 12.8296° N, 45.0300° E. It stands 2 metres (7 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Aden International Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 3,100 metres (10,171 feet). That is long enough for most passenger jets. Among the 7 airports of Yemen on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 3.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 08/26, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 08 is heading about 80° on the compass, and one using the other end about 260°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 2 metres above sea level, Aden International Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 7 airports of Yemen with a height in the data, it is the lowest. It is about 1,430 km north of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Aden International Airport there are 3 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest other large airport is Djibouti-Ambouli Airport (JIB) in Djibouti, about 248 km away.
