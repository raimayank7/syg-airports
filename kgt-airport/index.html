Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, KGT means Kangding Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: ZUKD. ICAO codes that begin with Z belong to China, Mongolia and North Korea. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 30.1425° N, 101.7387° E. It stands 4,280 metres (14,042 feet) above sea level. That is high for an airport. Thin air at this height means aircraft need a longer run to take off.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 15/33, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 15 is heading about 150° on the compass, and one using the other end about 330°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 4,280 metres, Kangding Airport stands higher than about 99% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 212 airports of China with a height in the data, it is number 4 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 59% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 3,350 km north of the equator.
