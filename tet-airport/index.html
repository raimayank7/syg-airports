Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, TET means Tete Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: FQTT. ICAO codes that begin with F belong to central and southern Africa. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 16.1048° S, 33.6402° E. It stands 160 metres (525 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 01/19, lies roughly south to north. An aircraft using the end marked 01 is heading about 10° on the compass, and one using the other end about 190°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 160 metres, Tete Airport stands higher than about 61% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 12 airports of Mozambique with a height in the data, it is number 4 from the top. It is about 1,790 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Tete Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest other large airport is Chileka International Airport (BLZ) in Malawi, about 150 km away.
