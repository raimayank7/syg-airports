Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, YAA means Anahim Lake Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: CAJ4. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 52.4515° N, 125.3038° W. It stands 1,108 metres (3,635 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Anahim Lake Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,198 metres (3,930 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 245 airports of Canada on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 159.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 13/31, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 13 is heading about 130° on the compass, and one using the other end about 310°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 1,108 metres, Anahim Lake Airport stands higher than about 91% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 255 airports of Canada with a height in the data, it is the highest. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 88% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 5,830 km north of the equator.
