Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, KVG means Kavieng Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: AYKV. ICAO codes that begin with A belong to the western South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 2.5794° S, 150.8080° E. It stands 2 metres (7 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Kavieng Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,704 metres (5,592 feet). That suits narrow-body jets and regional aircraft. Among the 32 airports of Papua New Guinea on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 9.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 12/30, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 12 is heading about 120° on the compass, and one using the other end about 300°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 2 metres above sea level, Kavieng Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 37 airports of Papua New Guinea with a height in the data, it is the lowest. It is about 290 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Kavieng Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest large airport is Nadzab Tomodachi International Airport (LAE), about 633 km away, roughly 1 h 15 min in the air.
