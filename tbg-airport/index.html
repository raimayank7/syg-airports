Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, TBG means Tabubil Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: AYTB. ICAO codes that begin with A belong to the western South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 5.2805° S, 141.2276° E. It stands 479 metres (1,570 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Tabubil Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,290 metres (4,232 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 32 airports of Papua New Guinea on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 22.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 14/32, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 14 is heading about 140° on the compass, and one using the other end about 320°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 479 metres, Tabubil Airport stands higher than about 80% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 37 airports of Papua New Guinea with a height in the data, it is number 9 from the top. It is about 590 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
