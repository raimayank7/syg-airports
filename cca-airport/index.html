Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, CCA means Chimore Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: SLHI. ICAO codes that begin with S belong to South America. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 16.9768° S, 65.1456° W. It stands 267 metres (875 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Sacaba, about 106 km away in a straight line.

Runways

Chimore Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,463 metres (4,801 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 15 airports of Bolivia on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 15.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 17/35, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 17 is heading about 170° on the compass, and one using the other end about 350°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 267 metres, Chimore Airport stands higher than about 70% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 16 airports of Bolivia with a height in the data, it is number 10 from the top. It is about 1,890 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Chimore Airport there are 6 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. The nearest large airport is Jorge Wilsterman International Airport (CBB), about 120 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
