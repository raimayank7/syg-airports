Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, AKU means Aksu Hongqipo Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: ZWAK. ICAO codes that begin with Z belong to China, Mongolia and North Korea. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 41.2625° N, 80.2917° E. It stands 1,163 metres (3,816 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 09/27, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 09 is heading about 90° on the compass, and one using the other end about 270°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 1,163 metres, Aksu Hongqipo Airport stands higher than about 92% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 212 airports of China with a height in the data, it is number 53 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 87% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 4,590 km north of the equator.
