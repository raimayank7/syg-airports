Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, BHS means Bathurst Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: YBTH. ICAO codes that begin with Y belong to Australia. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 33.4068° S, 149.6512° E. It stands 742 metres (2,435 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Sydney, about 153 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 17/35, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 17 is heading about 170° on the compass, and one using the other end about 350°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 742 metres, Bathurst Airport stands higher than about 86% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 152 airports of Australia with a height in the data, it is number 4 from the top. It is about 3,710 km south of the equator.
