Long Apung-Borneo Island, Kalimantan Utara (North Kalimantan), Indonesia

Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LPU means Long Apung Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: WAQL. ICAO codes that begin with W belong to Indonesia, Malaysia and nearby island countries. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 1.7069° N, 114.9699° E. It stands 743 metres (2,437 feet) above sea level. The nearest town in our data is Belaga, about 172 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 17/35, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 17 is heading about 170° on the compass, and one using the other end about 350°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 743 metres, Long Apung Airport stands higher than about 87% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 121 airports of Indonesia with a height in the data, it is number 11 from the top. It is about 190 km north of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
