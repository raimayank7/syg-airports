Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, BNE means Brisbane International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: YBBN. ICAO codes that begin with Y belong to Australia. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 27.3842° S, 153.1170° E. It stands 4 metres (13 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 01R/19L, lies roughly south to north. An aircraft using the end marked 01R is heading about 10° on the compass, and one using the other end about 190°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. All 2 runways here point the same way, so they can be used side by side.

Height and position

At 4 metres above sea level, Brisbane International Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 152 airports of Australia with a height in the data, it is number 139 from the top. It is about 3,040 km south of the equator.
