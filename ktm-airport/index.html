Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, KTM means Tribhuvan International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: VNKT. ICAO codes that begin with V belong to South Asia and mainland South-East Asia. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 27.6966° N, 85.3591° E. It stands 1,338 metres (4,390 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 02/20, lies roughly south to north. An aircraft using the end marked 02 is heading about 20° on the compass, and one using the other end about 200°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 1,338 metres, Tribhuvan International Airport stands higher than about 93% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 19 airports of Nepal with a height in the data, it is number 7 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 85% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 3,080 km north of the equator.
