Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LIF means Lifou Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: NWWL. ICAO codes that begin with N belong to the South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 20.7746° S, 167.2393° E. It stands 28 metres (92 feet) above sea level. The nearest town in our data is Dumbéa, about 174 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 12/30, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 12 is heading about 120° on the compass, and one using the other end about 300°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 28 metres, Lifou Airport stands higher than about 34% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 11 airports of New Caledonia with a height in the data, it is number 5 from the top. It is about 2,310 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
