Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LAO means Laoag International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: RPLI. ICAO codes that begin with R belong to East Asia, including Japan, Korea and the Philippines. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 18.1751° N, 120.5310° E. It stands 8 metres (25 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 01/19, lies roughly south to north. An aircraft using the end marked 01 is heading about 10° on the compass, and one using the other end about 190°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 8 metres above sea level, Laoag International Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 42 airports of Philippines with a height in the data, it is number 27 from the top. It is about 2,020 km north of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Laoag International Airport there are 3 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest other large airport is Clark International Airport / Clark Air Base (CRK), about 332 km away, roughly 55 min in the air.
