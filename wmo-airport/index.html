Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, WMO means White Mountain Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: PAWM. ICAO codes that begin with P belong to the North Pacific, including Alaska and Hawaii. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 64.6892° N, 163.4130° W. It stands 81 metres (267 feet) above sea level.

Runways

White Mountain Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 914 metres (3,000 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 675 airports of United States on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 632.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 15/33, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 15 is heading about 150° on the compass, and one using the other end about 330°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 81 metres, White Mountain Airport stands higher than about 50% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 645 airports of United States with a height in the data, it is number 351 from the top. It is about 7,190 km north of the equator, far enough from it for very long summer days and very short winter ones.
