Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, DTM means Dortmund Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: EDLW. ICAO codes that begin with E belong to northern Europe. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 51.5183° N, 7.6122° E. It stands 130 metres (425 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 06/24, lies roughly south-west to north-east. An aircraft using the end marked 06 is heading about 60° on the compass, and one using the other end about 240°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 130 metres, Dortmund Airport stands higher than about 57% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 38 airports of Germany with a height in the data, it is number 13 from the top. It is about 5,730 km north of the equator.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Dortmund Airport there are 34 other airports with scheduled flights, and 4 of them are within 100 km. That is a crowded patch of sky by world standards. The nearest other large airport is Düsseldorf Airport (DUS), about 64 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
