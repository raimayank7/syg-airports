Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, AXR means Arutua Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: NTGU. ICAO codes that begin with N belong to the South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 15.2483° S, 146.6170° W. It stands 3 metres (9 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Arutua Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,301 metres (4,268 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 31 airports of French Polynesia on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 16.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 10/28, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 10 is heading about 100° on the compass, and one using the other end about 280°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 3 metres above sea level, Arutua Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 38 airports of French Polynesia with a height in the data, it is number 24 from the top. It is about 1,700 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
