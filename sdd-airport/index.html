Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, SDD means Lubango Mukanka International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: FNUB. ICAO codes that begin with F belong to central and southern Africa. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 14.9248° S, 13.5767° E. It stands 1,761 metres (5,778 feet) above sea level. That is high for an airport. Thin air at this height means aircraft need a longer run to take off.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 10/28, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 10 is heading about 100° on the compass, and one using the other end about 280°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 1,761 metres, Lubango Mukanka International Airport stands higher than about 96% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 13 airports of Angola with a height in the data, it is the highest. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 81% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 1,660 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Lubango Mukanka International Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest large airport is Dr. Antonio Agostinho Neto International Airport (NBJ), about 653 km away, roughly 1 h 15 min in the air.
