Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, SRZ means El Trompillo Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: SLET. ICAO codes that begin with S belong to South America. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 17.8116° S, 63.1715° W. It stands 418 metres (1,371 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 16/34, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 16 is heading about 160° on the compass, and one using the other end about 340°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 418 metres, El Trompillo Airport stands higher than about 78% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 16 airports of Bolivia with a height in the data, it is number 7 from the top. It is about 1,980 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of El Trompillo Airport there are 3 other airports with scheduled flights, and 1 of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest large airport is Viru Viru International Airport (VVI), about 19 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
