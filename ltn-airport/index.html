Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LTN means London Luton Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: EGGW. ICAO codes that begin with E belong to northern Europe. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 51.8747° N, 0.3683° W. It stands 160 metres (526 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Islington, about 42 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 07/25, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 07 is heading about 70° on the compass, and one using the other end about 250°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

At 160 metres, London Luton Airport stands higher than about 61% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 53 airports of United Kingdom with a height in the data, it is number 3 from the top. It is about 5,770 km north of the equator.
