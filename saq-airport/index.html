Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, SAQ means San Andros Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: MYAN. ICAO codes that begin with M belong to Mexico and Central America. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 25.0538° N, 78.0490° W. It stands 2 metres (5 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Nassau, about 71 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 12/30, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 12 is heading about 120° on the compass, and one using the other end about 300°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 2 metres above sea level, San Andros Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 22 airports of Bahamas with a height in the data, it is number 21 from the top. It is about 2,790 km north of the equator.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of San Andros Airport there are 20 other airports with scheduled flights, and 3 of them are within 100 km. The nearest large airport is Lynden Pindling International Airport (NAS), about 59 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
