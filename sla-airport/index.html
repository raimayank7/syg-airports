Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, SLA means Martín Miguel de Güemes International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: SASA. ICAO codes that begin with S belong to South America. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 24.8560° S, 65.4862° W. It stands 1,246 metres (4,088 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 02/20, lies roughly south to north. An aircraft using the end marked 02 is heading about 20° on the compass, and one using the other end about 200°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 1,246 metres, Martín Miguel de Güemes International Airport stands higher than about 93% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 45 airports of Argentina with a height in the data, it is the highest. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 86% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 2,760 km south of the equator.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Martín Miguel de Güemes International Airport there are 3 other airports with scheduled flights, and 1 of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest other large airport is Gobernador Horacio Guzman International Airport (JUJ), about 65 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
