Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, NGB means Ningbo Lishe International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: ZSNB. ICAO codes that begin with Z belong to China, Mongolia and North Korea. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 29.8267° N, 121.4620° E. It stands 4 metres (13 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 13/31, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 13 is heading about 130° on the compass, and one using the other end about 310°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 4 metres above sea level, Ningbo Lishe International Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 212 airports of China with a height in the data, it is number 203 from the top. It is about 3,320 km north of the equator.
