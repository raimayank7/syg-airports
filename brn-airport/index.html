Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, BRN means Bern Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: LSZB. ICAO codes that begin with L belong to southern Europe, Israel and Turkey. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 46.9127° N, 7.4988° E. It stands 509 metres (1,671 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 14/32, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 14 is heading about 140° on the compass, and one using the other end about 320°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 5 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 509 metres, Bern Airport stands higher than about 81% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 5 airports of Switzerland with a height in the data, it is the highest. It is about 5,220 km north of the equator.
