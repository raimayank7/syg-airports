Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, KER means Ayatollah Hashemi Rafsanjani International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: OIKK. ICAO codes that begin with O belong to the Middle East, Pakistan and Afghanistan. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 30.2713° N, 56.9497° E. It stands 1,750 metres (5,741 feet) above sea level. That is high for an airport. Thin air at this height means aircraft need a longer run to take off.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 16L/34R, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 16L is heading about 160° on the compass, and one using the other end about 340°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. All 2 runways here point the same way, so they can be used side by side.

Height and position

At 1,750 metres, Ayatollah Hashemi Rafsanjani International Airport stands higher than about 96% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 45 airports of Iran with a height in the data, it is number 2 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 81% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 3,370 km north of the equator.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Ayatollah Hashemi Rafsanjani International Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and 1 of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest other large airport is Bandar Abbas International Airport (BND), about 344 km away, roughly 55 min in the air.
