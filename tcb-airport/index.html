Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, TCB means Treasure Cay Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: MYAT. ICAO codes that begin with M belong to Mexico and Central America. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 26.7453° N, 77.3913° W. It stands 2 metres (8 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Nassau, about 188 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 14/32, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 14 is heading about 140° on the compass, and one using the other end about 320°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 2 metres above sea level, Treasure Cay Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 22 airports of Bahamas with a height in the data, it is number 15 from the top. It is about 2,970 km north of the equator.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Treasure Cay Airport there are 15 other airports with scheduled flights, and 1 of them is within 100 km. The nearest large airport is Grand Bahama International Airport (FPO), about 131 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
