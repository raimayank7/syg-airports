Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, WLG means Wellington International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: NZWN. ICAO codes that begin with N belong to the South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 41.3268° S, 174.8069° E. It stands 12 metres (41 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 16/34, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 16 is heading about 160° on the compass, and one using the other end about 340°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 12 metres above sea level, Wellington International Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 28 airports of New Zealand with a height in the data, it is number 17 from the top. It is about 4,600 km south of the equator.
