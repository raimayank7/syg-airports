Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, YPR means Prince Rupert Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: CYPR. ICAO codes that begin with C belong to Canada. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 54.2861° N, 130.4450° W. It stands 35 metres (116 feet) above sea level. The nearest town in our data is Terrace, about 122 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 13/31, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 13 is heading about 130° on the compass, and one using the other end about 310°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 35 metres, Prince Rupert Airport stands higher than about 38% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 255 airports of Canada with a height in the data, it is number 190 from the top. It is about 6,040 km north of the equator.
