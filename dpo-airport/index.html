Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, DPO means Devonport Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: YDPO. ICAO codes that begin with Y belong to Australia. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 41.1697° S, 146.4300° E. It stands 10 metres (33 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 06/24, lies roughly south-west to north-east. An aircraft using the end marked 06 is heading about 60° on the compass, and one using the other end about 240°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 10 metres above sea level, Devonport Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 152 airports of Australia with a height in the data, it is number 115 from the top. It is about 4,580 km south of the equator.
