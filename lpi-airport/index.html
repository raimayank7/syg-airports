Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LPI means Linköping City Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: ESSL. ICAO codes that begin with E belong to northern Europe. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 58.4049° N, 15.6845° E. It stands 52 metres (172 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 11/29, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 11 is heading about 110° on the compass, and one using the other end about 290°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 52 metres, Linköping City Airport stands higher than about 44% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 38 airports of Sweden with a height in the data, it is number 23 from the top. It is about 6,490 km north of the equator, far enough from it for very long summer days and very short winter ones.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Linköping City Airport there are 20 other airports with scheduled flights, and 3 of them are within 100 km. The nearest other large airport is Stockholm Skavsta Airport (NYO), about 83 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
