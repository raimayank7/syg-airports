Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LUN means Kenneth Kaunda International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: FLKK. ICAO codes that begin with F belong to central and southern Africa. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 15.3308° S, 28.4527° E. It stands 1,152 metres (3,779 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 10/28, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 10 is heading about 100° on the compass, and one using the other end about 280°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 1,152 metres, Kenneth Kaunda International Airport stands higher than about 92% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 6 airports of Zambia with a height in the data, it is number 4 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 87% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 1,700 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Kenneth Kaunda International Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest other large airport is Simon Mwansa Kapwepwe International Airport (NLA), about 263 km away.
