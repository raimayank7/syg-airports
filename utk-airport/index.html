Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, UTK means Utirik Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: 03N. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 11.2222° N, 169.8514° E. It stands 1 metres (4 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Utirik Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 732 metres (2,400 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 8 airports of Marshall Islands on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 8.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 7/25, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 7 is heading about 70° on the compass, and one using the other end about 250°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 1 metre above sea level, Utirik Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 12 airports of Marshall Islands with a height in the data, it is number 11 from the top. It is about 1,250 km north of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Utirik Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest large airport is Marshall Islands International Airport (MAJ), about 488 km away, roughly 1 h 05 min in the air.
