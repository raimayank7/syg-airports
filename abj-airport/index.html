Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, ABJ means Félix-Houphouët-Boigny International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: DIAP. ICAO codes that begin with D belong to West Africa. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 5.2614° N, 3.9263° W. It stands 6 metres (21 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 03/21, lies roughly south-west to north-east. An aircraft using the end marked 03 is heading about 30° on the compass, and one using the other end about 210°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 6 metres above sea level, Félix-Houphouët-Boigny International Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. It is about 590 km north of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Félix-Houphouët-Boigny International Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest other large airport is Prempeh I International Airport (KMS) in Ghana, about 305 km away, roughly 50 min in the air.
