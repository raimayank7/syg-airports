Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, KYK means Karluk Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: PAKY. ICAO codes that begin with P belong to the North Pacific, including Alaska and Hawaii. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 57.5659° N, 154.4538° W. It stands 42 metres (137 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Karluk Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 610 metres (2,000 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 675 airports of United States on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 661.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 10/28, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 10 is heading about 100° on the compass, and one using the other end about 280°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 42 metres, Karluk Airport stands higher than about 40% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 645 airports of United States with a height in the data, it is number 407 from the top. It is about 6,400 km north of the equator, far enough from it for very long summer days and very short winter ones.
