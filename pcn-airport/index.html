Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, PCN means Picton Aerodrome. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: NZPN. ICAO codes that begin with N belong to the South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 41.3461° S, 173.9560° E. It stands 49 metres (161 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Wellington, about 69 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 18/36, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 18 is heading about 180° on the compass, and one using the other end about 360°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

At 49 metres, Picton Aerodrome stands higher than about 42% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 28 airports of New Zealand with a height in the data, it is number 8 from the top. It is about 4,600 km south of the equator.
