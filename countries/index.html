Which countries have the most?

By airports with scheduled flights in this data: United States (679), Canada (271), China (261), Australia (158), Russia (148), Brazil (129), Indonesia (121), India (116). Large countries with long distances and many remote towns, such as the United States, Canada, Australia and Russia, depend on air travel and have the most.

How to read the table

The first figure for each country is the number of airports that have a three-letter IATA code in the open data. The second is how many of those have scheduled airline flights, which is the number that matters to a traveller. The gap between the two is made up of airfields used by private, charter, cargo, medical and military flights, and of airports that have lost their airline service but kept their code. Open a country for the full list, the main airport, runway and height figures, and the nearest airports across the border.

The list in numbers

The data has 9,053 airports with a code in 239 countries and territories, and 4,133 of them have scheduled flights. The five countries at the top hold 37% of all airports with flights. At the other end, 49 countries and territories have exactly one airport with scheduled flights, so that one runway is their only air link with the world, and 5 have a coded airfield but no scheduled flights at all in this data.

Why the count differs from other lists

There is no single agreed number of airports for any country. Counts differ according to whether you include grass airstrips, military bases, heliports and seaplane bases, and on the day the data was taken. The figures here are simply what the OurAirports data held on 11 November 2026, counted the same way for every country.
