Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, JKG means Jönköping Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: ESGJ. ICAO codes that begin with E belong to northern Europe. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 01/19, lies roughly south to north. An aircraft using the end marked 01 is heading about 10° on the compass, and one using the other end about 190°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 226 metres, Jönköping Airport stands higher than about 67% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 38 airports of Sweden with a height in the data, it is number 9 from the top. It is about 6,420 km north of the equator, far enough from it for very long summer days and very short winter ones.
