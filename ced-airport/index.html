Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, CED means Ceduna Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: YCDU. ICAO codes that begin with Y belong to Australia. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 32.1306° S, 133.7100° E. It stands 23 metres (77 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Ceduna Airport has 2 runways in the data. The longest is 1,740 metres (5,709 feet). That suits narrow-body jets and regional aircraft. Among the 156 airports of Australia on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 64.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 11/29, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 11 is heading about 110° on the compass, and one using the other end about 290°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 23 metres, Ceduna Airport stands higher than about 31% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 152 airports of Australia with a height in the data, it is number 93 from the top. It is about 3,570 km south of the equator.

Other airports around it

Ceduna Airport is an isolated airport: no other airport with scheduled flights lies within 300 km of it. The nearest is Prominent Hill Airport (PXH), about 319 km away. The nearest large airport is Adelaide International Airport (ADL), about 546 km away, roughly 1 h 10 min in the air.
