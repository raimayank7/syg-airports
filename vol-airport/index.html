Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, VOL means Nea Anchialos National Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: LGBL. ICAO codes that begin with L belong to southern Europe, Israel and Turkey. How the two kinds of code differ.

The airport lies at 39.2196° N, 22.7943° E. It stands 25 metres (83 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Lárisa, about 56 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 08/26, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 08 is heading about 80° on the compass, and one using the other end about 260°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

At 25 metres, Nea Anchialos National Airport stands higher than about 33% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 39 airports of Greece with a height in the data, it is number 17 from the top. It is about 4,360 km north of the equator.
