Distance between airports

Type two airports by code, city or name. You get the distance over the globe and a rough flying time.

      How the distance is worked out

      The calculator uses the coordinates of the two airports and finds the shortest path over the surface of the globe, called the great-circle distance. On a flat map this path looks curved. That is why a flight from Delhi to New York passes far to the north, over Central Asia and the Arctic region, and not straight across the map.

      Why real flights are longer

      Aircraft follow air routes, avoid some airspace and bad weather, and circle when an airport is busy. The distance flown is usually a few percent more than the great-circle distance.

      About the flying time

      The time shown is a rough guide for a direct jet flight: the distance at about 830 km an hour, plus half an hour for climbing and landing. Winds change it a lot. Flights towards the east are often faster than the same route towards the west. A time is shown for any pair of airports, even when no airline flies directly between them.

      Kilometres, miles and nautical miles

      Aviation measures distance in nautical miles. One nautical mile is 1.852 kilometres, or about 1.15 ordinary miles.

      What people use it for

      The distance between two airports is useful for more than curiosity. Frequent-flyer schemes award points by distance flown. Travel claims and carbon estimates are often based on it. And it gives a quick sense of whether a journey is a two-hour hop or a fifteen-hour haul before you look at timetables. For all of these the great-circle figure is the usual starting point.
