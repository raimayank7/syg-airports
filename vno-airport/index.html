Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, VNO means Vilnius International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: EYVI. ICAO codes that begin with E belong to northern Europe. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 54.6341° N, 25.2858° E. It stands 198 metres (648 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 01/19, lies roughly south to north. An aircraft using the end marked 01 is heading about 10° on the compass, and one using the other end about 190°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 198 metres, Vilnius International Airport stands higher than about 64% of the 4,004 airports on this site. It is about 6,070 km north of the equator.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Vilnius International Airport there are 4 other airports with scheduled flights, and 1 of them is within 100 km. The nearest other large airport is Kaunas International Airport (KUN), about 85 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
