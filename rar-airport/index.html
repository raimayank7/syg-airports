Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, RAR means Rarotonga International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: NCRG. ICAO codes that begin with N belong to the South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 21.2027° S, 159.8060° W. It stands 6 metres (19 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 08/26, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 08 is heading about 80° on the compass, and one using the other end about 260°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 6 metres above sea level, Rarotonga International Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 6 airports of Cook Islands with a height in the data, it is number 4 from the top. It is about 2,360 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it
