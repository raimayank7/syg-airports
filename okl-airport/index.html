Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, OKL means Oksibil Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: WAJO. ICAO codes that begin with W belong to Indonesia, Malaysia and nearby island countries. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 4.9071° S, 140.6277° E. It stands 1,315 metres (4,315 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Oksibil Airport has 2 runways in the data. The longest is 870 metres (2,854 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 111 airports of Indonesia on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 106.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 11/29, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 11 is heading about 110° on the compass, and one using the other end about 290°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. All 2 runways here point the same way, so they can be used side by side.

Height and position

At 1,315 metres, Oksibil Airport stands higher than about 93% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 121 airports of Indonesia with a height in the data, it is number 6 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 85% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 550 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
