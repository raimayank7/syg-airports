Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LNO means Leonora Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: YLEO. ICAO codes that begin with Y belong to Australia. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 28.8781° S, 121.3150° E. It stands 371 metres (1,217 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 04/22, lies roughly south-west to north-east. An aircraft using the end marked 04 is heading about 40° on the compass, and one using the other end about 220°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 371 metres, Leonora Airport stands higher than about 76% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 152 airports of Australia with a height in the data, it is number 21 from the top. It is about 3,210 km south of the equator.
