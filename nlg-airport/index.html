Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, NLG means Nelson Lagoon Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: PAOU. ICAO codes that begin with P belong to the North Pacific, including Alaska and Hawaii. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 56.0075° N, 161.1600° W. It stands 4 metres (14 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Nelson Lagoon Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 1,220 metres (4,003 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 675 airports of United States on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 536.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 08/26, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 08 is heading about 80° on the compass, and one using the other end about 260°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 4 metres above sea level, Nelson Lagoon Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 645 airports of United States with a height in the data, it is number 602 from the top. It is about 6,230 km north of the equator, far enough from it for very long summer days and very short winter ones.
