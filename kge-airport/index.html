Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, KGE means Kaghau Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: AGKG. ICAO codes that begin with A belong to the western South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 7.3305° S, 157.5850° E. It stands 9 metres (30 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Kaghau Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 740 metres (2,428 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 18 airports of Solomon Islands on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 12.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 14/32, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 14 is heading about 140° on the compass, and one using the other end about 320°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 9 metres above sea level, Kaghau Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 18 airports of Solomon Islands with a height in the data, it is number 7 from the top. It is about 820 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Kaghau Airport there are 11 other airports with scheduled flights, and 1 of them is within 100 km. The nearest large airport is Honiara International Airport (HIR), about 358 km away, roughly 55 min in the air.
