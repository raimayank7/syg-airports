Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, ADQ means Kodiak Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: PADQ. ICAO codes that begin with P belong to the North Pacific, including Alaska and Hawaii. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 57.7500° N, 152.4940° W. It stands 24 metres (78 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 08/26, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 08 is heading about 80° on the compass, and one using the other end about 260°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 3 runways point in 3 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 24 metres, Kodiak Airport stands higher than about 32% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 645 airports of United States with a height in the data, it is number 460 from the top. It is about 6,420 km north of the equator, far enough from it for very long summer days and very short winter ones.
