Airports in New Zealand

Airport codes of New Zealand: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

New Zealand has 58 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 28 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Auckland (AKL), Christchurch (CHC), Wellington (WLG), Queenstown (ZQN). The longest runway is at Auckland International Airport: 3,635 metres. The highest is Taupo Airport, at 407 metres above sea level.

The airports of New Zealand in numbers

Of the 58 airports in New Zealand that carry an IATA code, 28 have scheduled airline flights and 30 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 4 large airports, 22 medium airports, 2 small airports. That places New Zealand at number 31 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Kaitaia Airport (KAT) and the most southerly is Invercargill Airport (IVC). The two that are farthest apart are Invercargill Airport and Kaitaia Airport: about 1,328 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 2 h 05 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Otago Region (3), Northland Region (3), Bay of Plenty Region (3).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 1 m at Dunedin International Airport to 407 m at Taupo Airport, and the average is about 74 metres above sea level. Of the 28 airports with runway figures, 2 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 17 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft. The main runway is hard (asphalt or concrete) at 24, unpaved (gravel, grass, earth, coral or similar) at 2, not stated at 2.

The codes used in New Zealand

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with NZ: the first letter stands for the South Pacific. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is W, which opens 10 of the 58 codes. In 79% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

Every airport of New Zealand with flights keeps the same clock time (zone Pacific/Auckland), so departure and arrival times on a domestic ticket can be subtracted directly.

From New Zealand to the rest of the world

Measured from Christchurch International Airport (CHC), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Norfolk Island International Airport (NLK, Norfolk Island) at 1,658 km, Lord Howe Island Airport (LDH, Australia) at 1,777 km, Hobart International Airport (HBA, Australia) at 2,023 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Delhi 12,555 km (about 15 h 40 min); Mumbai 12,219 km (about 15 h 15 min); Dubai 14,053 km (about 17 h 25 min); London Heathrow 18,984 km (about 23 h 20 min); New York JFK 14,680 km (about 18 h 10 min); Los Angeles 11,091 km (about 13 h 50 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.

Questions
