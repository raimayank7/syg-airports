Airports in Peru

Airport codes of Peru: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

Peru has 47 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 25 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Arequipa (AQP), Chiclayo (CIX), Cusco (CUZ), Iquitos (IQT), Juliaca (JUL), Lima (LIM), Pucallpa (PCL), Trujillo (TRU). The longest runway is at Inca Manco Capac International Airport: 4,200 metres. The highest is Inca Manco Capac International Airport, at 3,826 metres above sea level.

The airports of Peru in numbers

Of the 47 airports in Peru that carry an IATA code, 25 have scheduled airline flights and 22 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 8 large airports, 16 medium airports, 1 small airport. That places Peru at number 32 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Captain Pedro Canga Rodríguez International Airport (TBP) and the most southerly is Coronel FAP Carlos Ciriani Santa Rosa International Airport (TCQ). The two that are farthest apart are Captain Pedro Canga Rodríguez International Airport and Coronel FAP Carlos Ciriani Santa Rosa International Airport: about 1,952 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 2 h 50 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Cajamarca Region (2), Loreto Region (2), Piura Region (2).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 21 m at FAP Lieutenant Jaime Andres de Montreuil Morales Airport to 3,826 m at Inca Manco Capac International Airport, and the average is about 1,171 metres above sea level. 9 of them stand above 1,500 metres, where thin air makes take-off runs longer. Of the 25 airports with runway figures, 4 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 17 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 1 has nothing longer than 1,500 metres and takes mainly propeller aircraft.

The codes used in Peru

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with SP: the first letter stands for South America. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is A, which opens 7 of the 47 codes. In 87% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

Every airport of Peru with flights keeps the same clock time (zone America/Lima), so departure and arrival times on a domestic ticket can be subtracted directly.

From Peru to the rest of the world
