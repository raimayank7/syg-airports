Airports in Pakistan

Airport codes of Pakistan: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

Pakistan has 56 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 23 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Gurandani (GWD), Attock (ISB), Skardu (KDU), Karachi (KHI), Lahore (LHE), Faisalabad (LYP), Multan (MUX), Peshawar (PEW), Sialkot (SKT), Turbat (TUK), Quetta (UET). The longest runway is at Multan International Airport: 3,765 metres. The highest is Skardu International Airport, at 2,230 metres above sea level.

The airports of Pakistan in numbers

Of the 56 airports in Pakistan that carry an IATA code, 23 have scheduled airline flights and 33 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 11 large airports, 8 medium airports, 4 small airports. That places Pakistan at number 38 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Gilgit Airport (GIL) and the most southerly is Jinnah International Airport (KHI). The two that are farthest apart are New Gwadar International Airport and Skardu International Airport: about 1,674 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 2 h 30 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Punjab (8), Balochistan (6), Sindh (4).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 19 m at New Gwadar International Airport to 2,230 m at Skardu International Airport, and the average is about 586 metres above sea level. 2 of them stand above 1,500 metres, where thin air makes take-off runs longer. Of the 22 airports with runway figures, 8 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 7 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 1 has nothing longer than 1,500 metres and takes mainly propeller aircraft.

The codes used in Pakistan

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with OP: the first letter stands for the Middle East, Pakistan and Afghanistan. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is S, which opens 7 of the 56 codes. In 77% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

Every airport of Pakistan with flights keeps the same clock time (zone Asia/Karachi), so departure and arrival times on a domestic ticket can be subtracted directly.

From Pakistan to the rest of the world

Measured from Islamabad International Airport (ISB), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Srinagar International Airport (SXR, India) at 187 km, Jammu Airport (IXJ, India) at 210 km, Sri Guru Ram Das Ji International Airport (ATQ, India) at 276 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Delhi 688 km (about 1 h 20 min); Mumbai 1,608 km (about 2 h 25 min); Dubai 1,922 km (about 2 h 50 min); London Heathrow 6,053 km (about 7 h 50 min); New York JFK 11,083 km (about 13 h 50 min); Los Angeles 12,419 km (about 15 h 30 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.
