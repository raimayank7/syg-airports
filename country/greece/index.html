Airports in Greece

Airport codes of Greece: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

Greece has 42 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 39 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Spata-Artemida (ATH), Kerkyra (Corfu) (CFU), Souda (CHQ), Kefallinia Island (EFL), Heraklion (HER), Santorini Island (JTR), Kos Island (KGS), Kavala (KVA), Rhodes (RHO), Thessaloniki (SKG), Zakynthos (ZTH). The longest runway is at Athens Eleftherios Venizelos International Airport: 4,000 metres. The highest is Kastoria National Airport Aristotle, at 661 metres above sea level.

The airports of Greece in numbers

Of the 42 airports in Greece that carry an IATA code, 39 have scheduled airline flights and 3 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 11 large airports, 14 medium airports, 14 small airports. That places Greece at number 22 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Kavala Alexander the Great International Airport (KVA) and the most southerly is Sitia Airport (JSH). The two that are farthest apart are Corfu Ioannis Kapodistrias International Airport and Kastelorizo Airport: about 931 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 1 h 35 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in South Aegean (14), North Aegean (5), Ionian Islands (3).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 2 m at Corfu Ioannis Kapodistrias International Airport to 661 m at Kastoria National Airport Aristotle, and the average is about 91 metres above sea level. Of the 39 airports with runway figures, 5 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 15 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 11 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft.

The codes used in Greece

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with LG: the first letter stands for southern Europe, Israel and Turkey. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is J, which opens 10 of the 42 codes. In 52% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

The airports of Greece do not all keep the same time: they fall in 2 time zone areas (Athens, Istanbul). A ticket always shows the local time of each airport, so on a flight across the country the hours on the ticket do not simply subtract.

From Greece to the rest of the world

Measured from Athens Eleftherios Venizelos International Airport (ATH), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Adnan Menderes International Airport (ADB, Turkey) at 284 km, Balıkesir Koca Seyit Airport (EDO, Turkey) at 321 km, Çanakkale Airport (CKZ, Turkey) at 325 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Delhi 4,984 km (about 6 h 30 min); Mumbai 5,149 km (about 6 h 40 min); Dubai 3,273 km (about 4 h 25 min); London Heathrow 2,427 km (about 3 h 25 min); New York JFK 7,932 km (about 10 h 05 min); Los Angeles 11,127 km (about 13 h 55 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.

Questions

By size and runways in the open data, the main airport is Athens Eleftherios Venizelos International Airport, code ATH, serving Spata-Artemida.

How many airports are there in Greece?

Which foreign airport is nearest to Greece?
