Mozambique has 36 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 12 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Nampula (APL), Beira (BEW), Maputo (MPM), Tete (TET). The longest runway is at Maputo Airport: 3,660 metres. The highest is Lichinga Airport, at 1,373 metres above sea level.

The airports of Mozambique in numbers

Of the 36 airports in Mozambique that carry an IATA code, 12 have scheduled airline flights and 24 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 4 large airports, 7 medium airports, 1 small airport. That places Mozambique at number 66 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Pemba Airport (POL) and the most southerly is Maputo Airport (MPM). The two that are farthest apart are Maputo Airport and Pemba Airport: about 1,661 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 2 h 30 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Nampula Province (2), Inhambane Province (2), Sofala Province (1).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 9 m at Inhambane Airport to 1,373 m at Lichinga Airport, and the average is about 256 metres above sea level. Of the 12 airports with runway figures, 1 has a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 5 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 2 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft.

The codes used in Mozambique

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with FQ: the first letter stands for central and southern Africa. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is M, which opens 6 of the 36 codes. In 64% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

Every airport of Mozambique with flights keeps the same clock time (zone Africa/Maputo), so departure and arrival times on a domestic ticket can be subtracted directly.
