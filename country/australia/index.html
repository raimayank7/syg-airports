The airports of Australia in numbers

Of the 612 airports in Australia that carry an IATA code, 158 have scheduled airline flights and 454 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 16 large airports, 100 medium airports, 40 small airports, 2 seaplane bases. That places Australia at number 4 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Boigu Island Airport (GIC) and the most southerly is Hobart International Airport (HBA). The two that are farthest apart are Lord Howe Island Airport and Learmonth Airport: about 4,547 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 6 h 00 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Queensland (63), New South Wales (29), Western Australia (26).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 1 m at Coconut Island Airport to 1,298 m at Mount Hotham Airport, and the average is about 155 metres above sea level. Of the 156 airports with runway figures, 7 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 20 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 43 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft. The main runway is hard (asphalt or concrete) at 132, not stated at 14, unpaved (gravel, grass, earth, coral or similar) at 10.

The codes used in Australia

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with Y, the letter for Australia. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is M, which opens 61 of the 612 codes. In 77% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. How the two kinds of code work.

Clock times

The airports of Australia do not all keep the same time: they fall in 7 time zone areas (Brisbane, Sydney, Port Moresby, Melbourne, Perth, Adelaide and others). A ticket always shows the local time of each airport, so on a flight across the country the hours on the ticket do not simply subtract.

Questions

What is the main airport code of Australia?

By size and runways in the open data, the main airport is Sydney Kingsford Smith International Airport, code SYD, serving Sydney (Mascot).

How many airports are there in Australia?

612 airports in Australia have an IATA code in the open data, and 158 of them have scheduled airline flights.

Which foreign airport is nearest to Australia?

Measured from Sydney Kingsford Smith International Airport (SYD), the nearest airport with scheduled flights in another country is Norfolk Island International Airport (NLK) in Norfolk Island, about 1,678 km away in a straight line.
