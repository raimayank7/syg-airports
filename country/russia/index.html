Airports in Russia

Airport codes of Russia: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

Russia has 219 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 148 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Abakan (ABA), Sochi (AER), Astrakhan (ASF), Barnaul (BAX), Chelyabinsk (CEK), Moscow (DME), Nizhny Novgorod (GOJ), Grozny (GRV), Saratov (GSV), Chita (HTA), Tunoshna (IAR), Irkutsk (IKT) and 31 more. The longest runway is at Ulyanovsk Vostochny Airport: 5,000 metres. The highest is Chulman Airport, at 857 metres above sea level.

The airports of Russia in numbers

Of the 219 airports in Russia that carry an IATA code, 148 have scheduled airline flights and 71 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 43 large airports, 81 medium airports, 24 small airports. That places Russia at number 5 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Khatanga Airport (HTG) and the most southerly is Makhachkala Uytash International Airport (MCX). The two that are farthest apart are Gelendzhik Airport and Iturup Airport: about 7,882 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 10 h 00 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Sakha (Yakutia) Republic (23), Krasnoyarsk Krai (11), Khanty-Mansi Autonomous Okrug (8).

Heights and runways

Heights run from -20 m at Astrakhan Narimanovo Boris M. Kustodiev International Airport to 857 m at Chulman Airport, and the average is about 168 metres above sea level. Of the 136 airports with runway figures, 33 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 65 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 9 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft. The main runway is hard (asphalt or concrete) at 129, unpaved (gravel, grass, earth, coral or similar) at 4, not stated at 3.

The codes used in Russia

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with U, the letter for Russia and neighbouring states. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is K, which opens 26 of the 219 codes. In 68% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

The airports of Russia do not all keep the same time: they fall in 6 time zone areas (Moscow, Yekaterinburg, Krasnoyarsk, Vladivostok, Yakutsk, Irkutsk). A ticket always shows the local time of each airport, so on a flight across the country the hours on the ticket do not simply subtract.

From Russia to the rest of the world

Measured from Nizhny Novgorod / Strigino International Airport (GOJ), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Manshuk Mametova International Airport (URA, Kazakhstan) at 760 km, Mogilev Airport (MVQ, Belarus) at 905 km, Gomel Airport (GME, Belarus) at 922 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Delhi 4,042 km (about 5 h 20 min); Mumbai 4,785 km (about 6 h 15 min); Dubai 3,569 km (about 4 h 50 min); London Heathrow 2,898 km (about 4 h 00 min); New York JFK 7,755 km (about 9 h 50 min); Los Angeles 9,848 km (about 12 h 20 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.
