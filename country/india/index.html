Airport codes of India: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

India has 165 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 116 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Ahmedabad (AMD), Amritsar (ATQ), Bhubaneswar (BBI), Vadodara (BDQ), Bhopal (BHO), Bengaluru (BLR), Mumbai (BOM), Calicut (CCJ), Kolkata (CCU), Coimbatore (CJB), Kannur (CNN), Kochi (COK) and 31 more. The longest runway is at Indira Gandhi International Airport: 4,430 metres. The highest is Leh Kushok Bakula Rimpochee Airport, at 3,256 metres above sea level.

The airports of India in numbers

Of the 165 airports in India that carry an IATA code, 116 have scheduled airline flights and 49 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 43 large airports, 63 medium airports, 10 small airports. That places India at number 8 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Leh Kushok Bakula Rimpochee Airport (IXL) and the most southerly is Thiruvananthapuram International Airport (TRV). The two that are farthest apart are Veer Savarkar International Airport / INS Utkrosh and Srinagar International Airport: about 3,081 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 4 h 15 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Maharashtra (14), Uttar Pradesh (13), Gujarat (10).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 4 m at Veer Savarkar International Airport / INS Utkrosh to 3,256 m at Leh Kushok Bakula Rimpochee Airport, and the average is about 288 metres above sea level. 2 of them stand above 1,500 metres, where thin air makes take-off runs longer. Of the 114 airports with runway figures, 21 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 51 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 10 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft. The main runway is hard (asphalt or concrete) at 110, unpaved (gravel, grass, earth, coral or similar) at 4.

The codes used in India

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with VE, VO, VA or VI: the first letter stands for South Asia and mainland South-East Asia. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is I, which opens 27 of the 165 codes. In 61% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

Every airport of India with flights keeps the same clock time (zone Asia/Kolkata), so departure and arrival times on a domestic ticket can be subtracted directly.

From India to the rest of the world

Measured from Indira Gandhi International Airport (DEL), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Silgadi Doti Airport (SIH, Nepal) at 382 km, Allama Iqbal International Airport (LHE, Pakistan) at 419 km, Surkhet Airport (SKH, Nepal) at 443 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Dubai 2,182 km (about 3 h 10 min); London Heathrow 6,731 km (about 8 h 35 min); New York JFK 11,755 km (about 14 h 40 min); Los Angeles 12,877 km (about 16 h 00 min); Singapore 4,159 km (about 5 h 30 min); Tokyo Haneda 5,862 km (about 7 h 35 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.
