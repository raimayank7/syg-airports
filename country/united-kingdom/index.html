The airports of United Kingdom in numbers

Of the 98 airports in United Kingdom that carry an IATA code, 54 have scheduled airline flights and 44 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 17 large airports, 23 medium airports, 13 small airports, 1 heliport. That places United Kingdom at number 12 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Lerwick / Tingwall Airport (LWK) and the most southerly is St. Mary's Airport (ISC). The two that are farthest apart are St. Mary's Airport and Lerwick / Tingwall Airport: about 1,186 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 1 h 55 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Scotland (27), England (23), Northern Ireland (3).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 2 m at Barra Airport to 208 m at Leeds Bradford Airport, and the average is about 42 metres above sea level. Of the 53 airports with runway figures, 2 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 11 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 19 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft. The main runway is hard (asphalt or concrete) at 44, unpaved (gravel, grass, earth, coral or similar) at 8, not stated at 1.

The codes used in United Kingdom

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with EG: the first letter stands for northern Europe. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is B, which opens 15 of the 98 codes. In 65% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

Every airport of United Kingdom with flights keeps the same clock time (zone Europe/London), so departure and arrival times on a domestic ticket can be subtracted directly.
