Airports in Malaysia

Airport codes of Malaysia: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

Malaysia has 59 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 34 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Kota Kinabalu (BKI), Ipoh (IPH), Johor Bahru (JHB), Kuching (KCH), Sepang (KUL), Langkawi (LGK), Penang (PEN), Subang (SZB). The longest runway is at Kuala Lumpur International Airport: 4,124 metres. The highest is Bario Airport, at 1,021 metres above sea level.

The airports of Malaysia in numbers

Of the 59 airports in Malaysia that carry an IATA code, 34 have scheduled airline flights and 25 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 8 large airports, 18 medium airports, 8 small airports. That places Malaysia at number 27 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Kudat Airport (KUD) and the most southerly is Kuching International Airport (KCH). The two that are farthest apart are Langkawi International Airport and Tomanggong Airport: about 2,096 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 3 h 00 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Sarawak (14), Sabah (6), Kedah (2).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 2 m at Lawas Airport to 1,021 m at Bario Airport, and the average is about 94 metres above sea level. Of the 29 airports with runway figures, 8 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 9 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 9 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft. The main runway is hard (asphalt or concrete) at 26, not stated at 2, unpaved (gravel, grass, earth, coral or similar) at 1.

The codes used in Malaysia

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with WB or WM: the first letter stands for Indonesia, Malaysia and nearby island countries. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is L, which opens 11 of the 59 codes. In 83% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

The airports of Malaysia do not all keep the same time: they fall in 2 time zone areas (Kuching, Kuala Lumpur). A ticket always shows the local time of each airport, so on a flight across the country the hours on the ticket do not simply subtract.

From Malaysia to the rest of the world

Measured from Kuala Lumpur International Airport (KUL), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Pinang Kampai Airport (DUM, Indonesia) at 130 km, Sultan Syarif Kasim II International Airport / Roesmin Nurjadin AFB (PKU, Indonesia) at 256 km, Seletar Airport (XSP, Singapore) at 282 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Delhi 3,877 km (about 5 h 10 min); Mumbai 3,624 km (about 4 h 50 min); Dubai 5,548 km (about 7 h 10 min); London Heathrow 10,605 km (about 13 h 15 min); New York JFK 15,169 km (about 18 h 45 min); Los Angeles 14,163 km (about 17 h 35 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.
