Airports in Mexico

Airport codes of Mexico: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

Mexico has 99 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 63 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Acapulco (ACA), Aguascalientes (AGU), Silao (BJX), Ciudad Juárez (CJS), Culiacán (CUL), Cancún (CUN), Chihuahua (CUU), Cozumel (CZM), Guadalajara (GDL), Hermosillo (HMO), Huatulco (HUX), Loreto (LTO) and 17 more. The longest runway is at Felipe Ángeles International Airport: 4,500 metres. The highest is Adolfo López Mateos International Airport, at 2,580 metres above sea level.

The airports of Mexico in numbers

Of the 99 airports in Mexico that carry an IATA code, 63 have scheduled airline flights and 36 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 29 large airports, 30 medium airports, 4 small airports. That places Mexico at number 10 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is General Rodolfo Sánchez Taboada International Airport (MXL) and the most southerly is Tapachula International Airport (TAP). The two that are farthest apart are Cozumel International Airport and General Abelardo L. Rodriguez International Airport: about 3,259 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 4 h 25 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Baja California Sur (6), Tamaulipas (5), Sonora (4).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 3 m at Ciudad del Carmen International Airport to 2,580 m at Adolfo López Mateos International Airport, and the average is about 590 metres above sea level. 14 of them stand above 1,500 metres, where thin air makes take-off runs longer. Of the 62 airports with runway figures, 12 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 33 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets.

The codes used in Mexico

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with MM: the first letter stands for Mexico and Central America. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is C, which opens 18 of the 99 codes. In 77% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

The airports of Mexico do not all keep the same time: they fall in 8 time zone areas (Mexico City, Mazatlan, Merida, Monterrey, Hermosillo, Cancun and others). A ticket always shows the local time of each airport, so on a flight across the country the hours on the ticket do not simply subtract.

From Mexico to the rest of the world

Measured from Felipe Ángeles International Airport (NLU), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Brownsville South Padre Island International Airport (BRO, United States) at 704 km, McAllen Miller International Airport (MFE, United States) at 720 km, Valley International Airport (HRL, United States) at 734 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Delhi 14,628 km (about 18 h 05 min); Mumbai 15,607 km (about 19 h 20 min); Dubai 14,297 km (about 17 h 45 min); London Heathrow 8,874 km (about 11 h 10 min); New York JFK 3,335 km (about 4 h 30 min); Los Angeles 2,481 km (about 3 h 30 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.
