Airports in Canada

Airport codes of Canada: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

Canada has 477 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 271 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Edmonton (YEG), Hamilton (YHM), Halifax (YHZ), Kelowna (YLW), Ottawa (YOW), Quebec (YQB), Windsor (YQG), Montréal (YUL), Vancouver (YVR), Winnipeg (YWG), Saskatoon (YXE), Calgary (YYC) and 3 more. The longest runway is at Calgary International Airport: 4,267 metres. The highest is Anahim Lake Airport, at 1,108 metres above sea level.

The airports of Canada in numbers

Of the 477 airports in Canada that carry an IATA code, 271 have scheduled airline flights and 206 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 15 large airports, 122 medium airports, 115 small airports, 19 seaplane bases. That places Canada at number 2 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Grise Fiord Airport (YGZ) and the most southerly is Windsor International Airport (YQG). The two that are farthest apart are St. John's International Airport and Sandspit Airport: about 5,343 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 6 h 55 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in British Columbia (52), Ontario (51), Quebec (46).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 0 m at Ganges Seaplane Base to 1,108 m at Anahim Lake Airport, and the average is about 206 metres above sea level. Of the 245 airports with runway figures, 10 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 24 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 123 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft. The main runway is hard (asphalt or concrete) at 134, unpaved (gravel, grass, earth, coral or similar) at 105, not stated at 6.

The codes used in Canada

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with CY, CZ or CB: the first letter stands for Canada. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is Y, which opens 387 of the 477 codes. In 5% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. How the two kinds of code work.

Clock times

The airports of Canada do not all keep the same time: they fall in 3 time zone areas (Vancouver, Toronto, Edmonton). A ticket always shows the local time of each airport, so on a flight across the country the hours on the ticket do not simply subtract.

From Canada to the rest of the world

Measured from Toronto Pearson International Airport (YYZ), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Niagara Falls International Airport (IAG, United States) at 84 km, Buffalo Niagara International Airport (BUF, United States) at 109 km, Frederick Douglass Greater Rochester International Airport (ROC, United States) at 170 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Delhi 11,640 km (about 14 h 30 min); Mumbai 12,494 km (about 15 h 35 min); Dubai 11,083 km (about 13 h 50 min); London Heathrow 5,707 km (about 7 h 25 min); New York JFK 588 km (about 1 h 15 min); Los Angeles 3,494 km (about 4 h 45 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.

How many airports are there in Canada?

Which foreign airport is nearest to Canada?
