Airports in Philippines

Airport codes of Philippines: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

Philippines has 73 airports with an IATA code in the open data. 44 of them have scheduled airline flights and have their own page here. The large airports are Bacolod City (BCD), Cebu City/Lapu-Lapu City (CEB), Laguindingan (CGY), Mabalacat (CRK), Legazpi (DRP), Davao (DVO), General Santos (GES), Cabatuan (ILO), Kalibo (KLO), Laoag City (LAO), Manila (Pasay) (MNL), Puerto Princesa (PPS) and 3 more. The longest runway is at Ninoy Aquino International Airport: 3,737 metres. The highest is General Santos International Airport, at 154 metres above sea level.

The airports of Philippines in numbers

Of the 73 airports in Philippines that carry an IATA code, 44 have scheduled airline flights and 29 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 15 large airports, 24 medium airports, 5 small airports. That places Philippines at number 20 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Basco Airport (BSO) and the most southerly is Sanga Sanga Airport (TWT). The two that are farthest apart are Basco Airport and Sanga Sanga Airport: about 1,730 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 2 h 35 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Palawan Province (5), Surigao del Norte Province (2), Aklan Province (2).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 2 m at Pagadian Airport to 154 m at General Santos International Airport, and the average is about 28 metres above sea level. Of the 43 airports with runway figures, 4 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 8 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 11 have nothing longer than 1,500 metres and take mainly propeller aircraft. The main runway is hard (asphalt or concrete) at 42, unpaved (gravel, grass, earth, coral or similar) at 1.

The codes used in Philippines

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with RP: the first letter stands for East Asia, including Japan, Korea and the Philippines. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is C, which opens 12 of the 73 codes. In 67% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

Every airport of Philippines with flights keeps the same clock time (zone Asia/Manila), so departure and arrival times on a domestic ticket can be subtracted directly.

From Philippines to the rest of the world

Measured from Ninoy Aquino International Airport (MNL), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Lanyu Airport (KYD, Taiwan) at 838 km, Kaohsiung International Airport (KHH, Taiwan) at 900 km, Taitung Airport (TTT, Taiwan) at 917 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Delhi 4,773 km (about 6 h 15 min); Mumbai 5,136 km (about 6 h 40 min); Dubai 6,905 km (about 8 h 50 min); London Heathrow 10,767 km (about 13 h 30 min); New York JFK 13,696 km (about 17 h 00 min); Los Angeles 11,740 km (about 14 h 40 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.
