Airport codes of China: every airport with an IATA code, with its city. Type to filter.

The airports of China in numbers

Of the 319 airports in China that carry an IATA code, 261 have scheduled airline flights and 58 do not. By kind, the airports with flights are: 69 large airports, 157 medium airports, 35 small airports. That places China at number 3 among the 234 countries and territories on this site, counted by airports with scheduled flights.

Where they are

The most northerly airport with flights is Mohe Gulian Airport (OHE) and the most southerly is Sanya Phoenix International Airport (SYX). The two that are farthest apart are Fuyuan Dongji Airport and Ngari Gunsa Airport: about 4,835 km in a straight line, which would take roughly 6 h 20 min in a jet. By state or region, the most airports with flights are in Xinjiang Autonomous Region (27), Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region (18), Sichuan Province (18).

Heights and runways

Heights run from 2 m at Foshan Shadi Airport to 4,411 m at Daocheng Yading Airport, and the average is about 843 metres above sea level. 37 of them stand above 1,500 metres, where thin air makes take-off runs longer. Of the 256 airports with runway figures, 82 have a runway of 3,200 metres or more, long enough for the largest long-distance aircraft; 161 more reach 2,300 metres, enough for most passenger jets; 1 has nothing longer than 1,500 metres and takes mainly propeller aircraft. The main runway is hard (asphalt or concrete) at 156, not stated at 99, unpaved (gravel, grass, earth, coral or similar) at 1.

The codes used in China

The four-letter ICAO codes used by pilots here begin with Z, the letter for China, Mongolia and North Korea. Among the three-letter IATA codes, the most common first letter is H, which opens 37 of the 319 codes. In 60% of them the code starts with the same letter as the city or airport name. Clock times

The airports of China do not all keep the same time: they fall in 2 time zone areas (Shanghai, Urumqi). A ticket always shows the local time of each airport, so on a flight across the country the hours on the ticket do not simply subtract.

From China to the rest of the world

Measured from Shanghai Pudong International Airport (PVG), the nearest airports with scheduled flights in other countries are Jeju International Airport (CJU, South Korea) at 513 km, Matsu Beigan Airport (MFK, Taiwan) at 575 km, Matsu Nangan Airport (LZN, Taiwan) at 583 km. Straight-line distances to some major airports: Delhi 4,293 km (about 5 h 40 min); Mumbai 5,064 km (about 6 h 35 min); Dubai 6,448 km (about 8 h 15 min); London Heathrow 9,241 km (about 11 h 40 min); New York JFK 11,874 km (about 14 h 50 min); Los Angeles 10,415 km (about 13 h 05 min). These are distances over the globe with a rough time for a direct jet. They do not mean that a direct flight exists.
