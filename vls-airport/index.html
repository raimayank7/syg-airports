Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, VLS means Valesdir Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: NVSV. ICAO codes that begin with N belong to the South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 16.7961° S, 168.1770° E. It stands 3 metres (10 feet) above sea level. The nearest town in our data is Port-Vila, about 106 km away in a straight line.

Runways

Valesdir Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 800 metres (2,625 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 16 airports of Vanuatu on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 9.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 15/33, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 15 is heading about 150° on the compass, and one using the other end about 330°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 3 metres above sea level, Valesdir Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. Among the 17 airports of Vanuatu with a height in the data, it is number 14 from the top. It is about 1,870 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Valesdir Airport there are 13 other airports with scheduled flights, and 5 of them are within 100 km. The nearest large airport is Bauerfield International Airport (VLI), about 102 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
