Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, SHY means Shinyanga Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: HTSY. ICAO codes that begin with H belong to east and north-east Africa. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 3.6093° S, 33.5035° E. It stands 1,158 metres (3,800 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 11/29, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 11 is heading about 110° on the compass, and one using the other end about 290°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 1,158 metres, Shinyanga Airport stands higher than about 92% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 16 airports of Tanzania with a height in the data, it is number 6 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 87% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 400 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Shinyanga Airport there are 5 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. The nearest large airport is Mwanza International Airport (MWZ), about 144 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
