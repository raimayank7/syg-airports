Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, ORU means Juan Mendoza International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: SLOR. ICAO codes that begin with S belong to South America. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 17.9562° S, 67.0758° W. It stands 3,704 metres (12,152 feet) above sea level. That is high for an airport. Thin air at this height means aircraft need a longer run to take off.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 18/36, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 18 is heading about 180° on the compass, and one using the other end about 360°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 3,704 metres, Juan Mendoza International Airport stands higher than about 99% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 16 airports of Bolivia with a height in the data, it is number 2 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 63% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 2,000 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.
