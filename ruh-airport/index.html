Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, RUH means King Khalid International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: OERK. ICAO codes that begin with O belong to the Middle East, Pakistan and Afghanistan. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 24.9576° N, 46.6988° E. It stands 625 metres (2,049 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 15L/33R, lies roughly north-west to south-east. An aircraft using the end marked 15L is heading about 150° on the compass, and one using the other end about 330°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. All 2 runways here point the same way, so they can be used side by side.

Height and position

At 625 metres, King Khalid International Airport stands higher than about 84% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 29 airports of Saudi Arabia with a height in the data, it is number 16 from the top. It is about 2,780 km north of the equator.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of King Khalid International Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest other large airport is Al-Ahsa International Airport (HOF), about 283 km away.
