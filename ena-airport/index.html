Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, ENA means Kenai Municipal Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: PAEN. ICAO codes that begin with P belong to the North Pacific, including Alaska and Hawaii. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 60.5709° N, 151.2452° W. It stands 30 metres (99 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Anchorage, about 102 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 02L/20R, lies roughly south to north. An aircraft using the end marked 02L is heading about 20° on the compass, and one using the other end about 200°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. All 3 runways here point the same way, so they can be used side by side.
