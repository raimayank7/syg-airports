Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, OFU means Ofu Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: NSAS. ICAO codes that begin with N belong to the South Pacific. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 14.1844° S, 169.6700° W. It stands 3 metres (9 feet) above sea level. The nearest town in our data is Pago Pago, about 112 km away in a straight line.

Runways

Ofu Airport has 1 runway in the data. The longest is 610 metres (2,000 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 08/26, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 08 is heading about 80° on the compass, and one using the other end about 260°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 3 metres above sea level, Ofu Airport is close to sea level, like most airports built on coasts and river plains. It is about 1,580 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Ofu Airport there are 3 other airports with scheduled flights, and 1 of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest large airport is Pago Pago International Airport (PPG), about 113 km away, close enough to reach by road in most cases.
