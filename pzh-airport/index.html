Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, PZH means Zhob Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: OPZB. ICAO codes that begin with O belong to the Middle East, Pakistan and Afghanistan. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 31.3584° N, 69.4636° E. It stands 1,441 metres (4,728 feet) above sea level. The nearest large city in our data is Taunsa, about 135 km away in a straight line.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 10/28, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 10 is heading about 100° on the compass, and one using the other end about 280°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it.

Height and position

At 1,441 metres, Zhob Airport stands higher than about 94% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 23 airports of Pakistan with a height in the data, it is number 5 from the top. In a standard atmosphere the air pressure at this height is about 84% of the pressure at sea level. Engines and wings both work less well in thinner air, so the same aircraft needs more runway here than at the coast. It is about 3,490 km north of the equator.
