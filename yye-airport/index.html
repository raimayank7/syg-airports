Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, YYE means Fort Nelson Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: CYYE. ICAO codes that begin with C belong to Canada. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 58.8364° N, 122.5970° W. It stands 382 metres (1,253 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Fort Nelson Airport has 2 runways in the data. The longest is 1,951 metres (6,400 feet). That suits narrow-body jets and regional aircraft. Among the 245 airports of Canada on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 57.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 04/22, lies roughly south-west to north-east. An aircraft using the end marked 04 is heading about 40° on the compass, and one using the other end about 220°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 382 metres, Fort Nelson Airport stands higher than about 76% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 255 airports of Canada with a height in the data, it is number 40 from the top. It is about 6,540 km north of the equator, far enough from it for very long summer days and very short winter ones.

Other airports around it

Fort Nelson Airport is an isolated airport: no other airport with scheduled flights lies within 300 km of it. The nearest is Fort St John / North Peace Regional Airport (YXJ), about 309 km away. The nearest large airport is Edmonton International Airport (YEG), about 830 km away, roughly 1 h 30 min in the air.
