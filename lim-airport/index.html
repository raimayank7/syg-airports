Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LIM means Jorge Chávez International Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: SPJC. ICAO codes that begin with S belong to South America. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 12.0219° S, 77.1143° W. It stands 34 metres (113 feet) above sea level.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 16L/34R, lies roughly north to south. An aircraft using the end marked 16L is heading about 160° on the compass, and one using the other end about 340°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. All 2 runways here point the same way, so they can be used side by side.

Height and position

At 34 metres, Jorge Chávez International Airport stands higher than about 38% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 25 airports of Peru with a height in the data, it is number 21 from the top. It is about 1,340 km south of the equator, within the tropics, where days are close to twelve hours long all year.

Other airports around it

Within 300 km of Jorge Chávez International Airport there are 2 other airports with scheduled flights, and none of them is within 100 km. That is few: the region is thinly served by air. The nearest other large airport is Capitán FAP Carlos Martínez de Pinillos International Airport (TRU), about 489 km away, roughly 1 h 05 min in the air.
