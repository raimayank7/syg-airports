Airport codes

On a ticket or a baggage tag, LHG means Lightning Ridge Airport. Pilots and air traffic controllers use a different, four-letter ICAO code: YLRD. ICAO codes that begin with Y belong to Australia. How the two kinds of code differ.

Where it is

The airport lies at 29.4529° S, 147.9771° E. It stands 165 metres (540 feet) above sea level.

Runways

Lightning Ridge Airport has 2 runways in the data. The longest is 1,406 metres (4,613 feet). That is a short runway, used mostly by propeller aircraft. Among the 156 airports of Australia on this site with runway data, its longest runway ranks number 126.

Which way the runway points

The longest runway, 10/28, lies roughly west to east. An aircraft using the end marked 10 is heading about 100° on the compass, and one using the other end about 280°. Which end is in use on a given day depends on the wind, since aircraft land and take off into it. The 2 runways point in 2 different directions, which gives a choice when the wind swings round.

Height and position

At 165 metres, Lightning Ridge Airport stands higher than about 61% of the 4,004 airports on this site. Among the 152 airports of Australia with a height in the data, it is number 54 from the top. It is about 3,270 km south of the equator.
